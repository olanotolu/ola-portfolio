## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** This is genuinely authored for the product, not category-interchangeable. Monument-scale serif wordmarks, blur-as-depth, crosshair cursor, image-preview-on-hover, and the black preloader that cycles the visitor through the work before the wordmark blooms in — none of this would survive being transplanted onto another portfolio. The world is a coherent "gallery of monuments" and it reads instantly. The main specificity risk is that the two subpages (Research, Writing) relax into a conventional list layout; they are competent but not in the same voice as the home page.

**Deterministic scan:** Detector (detect.mjs) over app/page.tsx, app/research/page.tsx, app/writing/page.tsx — 0 findings after aligning DESIGN.md's type ramp with the real scale (17 advisory findings initially, all same rule: font sizes off the ramp; fixed by documenting the true scale).

**Visual overlays:** Browser visualization unavailable — this environment cannot persist a background dev server (nohup/setsid attempts both terminated at command end); fallback signal: static source review + detector.

## Overall Impression

The home page is the strongest thing here: a bold, coherent, memorable statement that does its job of making Ola read as a builder with a thesis. The single biggest opportunity is making the interactive promise hold everywhere: the mobile scroll interaction is fragile (one project's image never highlights due to a sibling-selection bug), the 404 page betrays the world entirely, and the click affordance is split. Fix those and the system is airtight.

## What's Working

1. **The monument interaction.** Giant serif name → siblings blur → image preview blooms bottom-right. It's distinctive, on-brand, and technically clean on desktop.
2. **The preloader as a moment.** Black field, giant white wordmark, work thumbnails cycling — it turns a loading wait into the first gallery impression, and the sessionStorage skip respects returning visitors.
3. **Restraint with a point.** No cards, no color (green/red reserved for exactly two wordmarks), hairline dividers only — every element earns its place.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The mobile project highlight has a real bug — Education never highlights
- **What:** The mobile scroll handler activates the image via `link.nextElementSibling`, but for the Education project a Harvard-shield div sits between the name and its image, so its image never becomes visible.
- **Why it matters:** Mobile is where hover doesn't exist; the scroll-highlight is the only preview mechanism, and it silently fails for one of six projects.
- **Fix:** Use the same `querySelector('[data-for=slug]')` lookup the desktop hover uses.
- **Suggested command:** $impeccable adapt

### [P1] 404 page is off-brand
- **What:** Unknown routes render Next.js's default 404 — system font, centered text, nothing of the monument world.
- **Why it matters:** Every dead link, typo, or stale share lands on a page that contradicts the brand; visitors get no way back to the thesis.
- **Fix:** Custom app/not-found.tsx in the monument voice (giant serif "404", blurred-out, link home).
- **Suggested command:** $impeccable harden

### [P2] Click affordance is inconsistent
- **What:** The four linked projects use a pointer cursor; everything else on the site (including all other links) uses the crosshair. Users can't tell which names are links without hovering.
- **Why it matters:** The crosshair is the site's deliberate "everything is a target" language; splitting it creates noise and reads as accidental.
- **Fix:** Decide one language — per the design doctrine, keep crosshair everywhere (links included).
- **Suggested command:** $impeccable polish

### [P2] Research topics render as a 10-item serif wall
- **What:** Ten topic tags in 20–26px serif, flex-wrapped, no grouping or hierarchy — the one decision point on the page with more than 4 options.
- **Why it matters:** Reads as a list dump rather than an agenda; dilutes the monument voice on the page that should feel most like a thesis.
- **Fix:** Either trim the visible set to the strongest 4–6 (content decision for the owner), or present as a single-line marquee to keep the serif voice.
- **Suggested command:** $impeccable distill

### [P3] Duplicate email affordances + micro-labels
- **What:** Email appears in the hero nav, as "Newsletter (200+ subscribers)" right below it, and again in the footer. Hero micro-labels sit at 10px.
- **Why it matters:** Minor redundancy; three exits to the same inbox cheapen the contact moment. 10px text is borderline legible on small screens.
- **Fix:** Consolidate contact into one nav item + footer; bump micro-labels to 11px on mobile if legibility is a concern.
- **Suggested command:** $impeccable clarify

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer):** The crosshair cursor makes everything look non-clickable; project names give no link affordance until hover. On mobile, tapping a name opens a new tab with no indicator — Jordan may feel the site "left" without warning. The 404 page (default Next) would utterly break the spell.

**Riley (Stress Tester):** Types a wrong URL → default Next 404, off-brand. The "JOHN DOE: Zero-Raw-Context AI Orchestration" paper title reads as an unfinished placeholder. SessionStorage preloader skip is a nice touch. Hover images on desktop and fixed bottom-right images on mobile can overlap the next name row while scrolling.

**Casey (Mobile, distracted):** Hero nav is top-right in 10px type — hard to hit one-handed. Project names are enormous (great targets). The 4.4s preloader on first load on a slow connection is a long wait with images loading eagerly.

## Minor Observations

- About section is one paragraph — thin relative to the ambition of the rest; worth growing.
- "JOHN DOE" title looks like a placeholder; confirm intent.
- Paper/Essay rows blur the whole row on hover, which blurs the title you're about to read — charming but borderline.
- The Harvard shield div between link and image is the exact structural trap that caused the mobile bug.
- Footer repeats the nav Email link.
- Research page voice is flatter than the home page; topics could carry more of the thesis energy.

## Questions to Consider

- What if the hero cycled its giant word each visit — OlaOlu., BUILD, IMPOSSIBLE, ORDINARY — as a slow rotating thesis?
- Should the project image previews exist inline on mobile (beneath the active name) instead of floating bottom-right?
- Is "personal research institution" the right frame, or should the thesis statement own the hero alone?
- Would the 10 research topics land harder as a single-line marquee in the monument type?
